# DeTLeng Upwork Growth Playbook

## The Rule Most Freelancers Miss

Clients do not buy SQL.

Clients do not buy ETL.

Clients do not buy BigQuery.

Clients buy:

* Reliable reporting
* Accurate KPIs
* Automated workflows
* Faster business decisions
* Trustworthy data

Every proposal, profile section, and conversation should connect technical work to business outcomes.

---

# Positioning Before Proposals

## Never Compete as a Generic Freelancer

Bad Positioning:

"I am a Data Engineer with SQL, Python, ETL, Power BI and BigQuery experience."

Strong Positioning:

"I help organizations transform fragmented, unreliable data into analytics-ready datasets that support accurate reporting, KPI tracking, and business decision-making."

Clients remember outcomes.

They forget tool lists.

---

# Build a Specialist Identity

Choose a primary category.

Examples:

* ETL & ELT Development
* BigQuery Consulting
* Data Warehouse Development
* Analytics Engineering
* Reporting Automation
* Data Quality & Validation

A specialist is easier to hire than a generalist.

---

# Proposal Strategy

## Never Start With Yourself

Bad:

"Hello, I am a Data Engineer with 5 years of experience."

Better:

"I noticed your reporting process depends on data coming from multiple systems. In situations like this, the biggest risk is usually inconsistent business logic across sources, which creates unreliable KPI reporting."

The client should feel understood before they learn about you.

---

# The 70 / 20 / 10 Proposal Framework

70% Client Problem

* Current challenge
* Business impact
* Risks
* Desired outcome

20% Solution

* High-level approach
* Expected deliverables
* Timeline assumptions

10% Credibility

* Relevant experience
* Similar projects
* Technical expertise

Most freelancers reverse this.

---

# Apply Selectively

Never chase volume.

Apply only when:

* Problem is clearly defined
* Budget is realistic
* Client has hiring history
* Project matches DeTLeng services

A focused pipeline beats mass applications.

---

# Create Micro-Authority

Publish content regularly:

* ETL mistakes businesses make
* Data quality failures
* KPI consistency problems
* Reporting automation
* BigQuery optimization
* Analytics Engineering

Clients trust visible expertise.

---

# Build Trust Fast

Every client fears:

* Missed deadlines
* Poor communication
* Broken systems
* Technical complexity

Reduce fear by explaining:

* What will happen
* When it will happen
* How progress will be measured

Clarity closes deals.

---

# Sell Assessments First

Many clients do not need implementation immediately.

Offer:

## Data Assessment

Deliverables:

* Current-state review
* Data quality findings
* Pipeline risks
* Architecture recommendations
* Roadmap

Small engagements often become larger projects.

---

# Use Business Language

Avoid:

* Airflow
* dbt
* Spark
* Kafka
* Medallion
* Star Schema

Until needed.

Start with:

* Reliable reporting
* KPI consistency
* Data accuracy
* Reduced manual work
* Faster decisions

Business language gets replies.

Technical language gets ignored.

---

# Communication Standard

Reply within hours.

Provide updates before clients ask.

Summarize decisions in writing.

Document everything.

Reliability is a competitive advantage.

---

# Win Without Reviews

If reviews are limited:

* Publish case studies
* Show architecture diagrams
* Share methodology
* Explain process clearly
* Demonstrate expertise publicly

Clients hire confidence before reviews.

# DeTLeng Proposal Opening Template

Hello,

I reviewed your project and the challenge appears to be less about reporting and more about creating a reliable data foundation.

When data comes from multiple systems, the biggest issues are usually inconsistent business logic, duplicate records, manual processing, and unreliable KPI calculations.

My approach would be to first validate data quality, identify transformation requirements, and then build an analytics-ready dataset that supports accurate reporting and long-term maintainability.

Could you share:

1. Current data sources?
2. Reporting platform?
3. Expected KPIs?
4. Existing data warehouse (if any)?

Once I understand the architecture, I can propose the most efficient implementation path.

Regards,
DeTLeng
Data Engineering • ETL • Analytics Engineering
